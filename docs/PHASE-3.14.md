## 1. Sources and scope

* **ROADMAP 3.14:** cashboxes and bank accounts per branch; receipt and payment vouchers (initially UNALLOCATED); transfers between cash and bank accounts; ledger integration; approval. Acceptance: a voucher posts once, balances reconcile to the ledger, branch authorization. Mandatory tests: PostgreSQL and web tests. Migrations: company and system. Risk: High.
* **SRS:** 3 (each branch has its own cashboxes); 4 (RBAC by branch, approval policies by type, value and branch, separation of duties, posted documents are immutable); 5 (balanced atomic journals, fiscal periods); 7.1 (receipt and payment vouchers; numbering per company, branch, type and fiscal year, official number at approval, a cancelled number is never reused); 10 (cashboxes, banks, transfers and vouchers; allocation to invoices belongs to 3.17); 19.2 (one effect per business event), 19.3 (no change to a posted document), 19.5 (no posting into Final Closed periods); 21.6 (a voucher allocation, a bank match or a retry after a disconnection never creates a duplicate receipt or entry).
* **RR-4 (cash account movement report):** opening, debit, credit, running and closing balance per cashbox or cash account; filters by account, branch, date and transaction type; each line navigates to its voucher or journal; the closing balance equals the ledger balance of the account.
* **Out of scope here (other phases, with their roadmap numbers):** allocation of vouchers to invoices, advances and credit notes (3.17); foreign-exchange differences (3.18); cheques (3.19); bank statement import and reconciliation (3.20); inter-branch settlement accounting (3.22); opening balances (3.27); recurring vouchers (3.21); direct bank integration (not mandatory in R1, SRS 11).

## 3. Design principles (each proven by a test in section 8)

1. **One effect per business event.** Key `cashvoucher:<voucher id>` (and `cashtransfer:<id>`) is the unique source key of the journal entry; a retry or a concurrent approval returns the original or refuses, never duplicates (SRS 19.2, 21.6).
2. **Posted means immutable.** A voucher or transfer that has an accounting effect cannot be edited, cancelled or deleted; PostgreSQL refuses UPDATE of identity and DELETE or TRUNCATE; correction is a reversal linked to the original (SRS 19.3).
3. **The ledger is the source of truth for balances.** A cash or bank account's balance is read from its GL account; nothing here stores a running balance (RR-4: closing balance equals the ledger balance).
4. **Company-scoped, branch-authorized, RBAC-checked on the server.** Both clients use the same API; no frontend-only security; no cross-company visibility (SRS 19.4).
5. **Fail closed.** A missing account, rule, open period, rate or configuration yields a refusal or an explicit pending state with a reason, never a guess.
6. **Separation of duties is not configurable away.** The approver differs from the creator, last editor and submitter (SRS 4); the automatic path never bypasses approval.
7. **Unallocated now, allocated later.** A voucher records WHO paid or was paid (an optional party reference) and a status "unallocated"; allocation to invoices (3.17) will add links without changing a posted voucher or its entry (SRS 10: linking never creates a duplicate receipt entry).

## 4. Data model sketch (logical; opaque physical names are generated by `tools/schema/company-00NN-cash-bank.cjs` with a `--check` CI step, like earlier phases)

* `CashAccount`: id, kind (cashbox or bank), code (unique per company, stable), names (Arabic or English), branch (required), the bound GL account (unique: one GL account serves one cash or bank account), currency, optional bank details (bank name, bank branch name, account number; the ACCOUNT NUMBER is sensitive per Q8: stored per the Q8 storage default, exposed only masked unless the viewer holds the sensitive permission), active flag, revision, audit fields. Never deleted. **Frozen once used:** the bound GL account and the branch cannot change once ANY voucher or transfer references the account (a different binding means a new cash or bank account), so history and RR-4 never read a different ledger account. **Deactivation** is refused while an OPEN voucher or transfer exists, where OPEN means: not yet approved, OR approved and its journal entry is neither posted nor cancelled. A masked value sent back by a client (for example an edit form that did not reveal the number) is recognised as "unchanged" and NEVER overwrites the stored number; replacing the number needs the full new value and the update permission.
* `CashVoucher`: id, kind (receipt or payment), cash or bank account, branch (the account's), date, party reference (optional), reference text, note, status (draft, submitted, approved, rejected, cancelled), official number (at approval), idempotency key, creator, last editor, submitter, approver, revision, counter lines.
* `CashVoucherLine`: voucher, line number, counter account (postable), amount in the voucher currency, description, optional cost-center allocation as the journal supports it (SRS 5); the line's branch is ALWAYS the voucher's branch (Q11).
* `CashTransfer`: id, from account, to account, date, amount, reference, status as vouchers, official number at approval, idempotency key, actors, revision.
* Append-only `CashEvent` rows (created, submitted, approved, rejected, cancelled, journal created, reversed) per document for the history tab.
* Link rows to the produced journal entry (document id, entry id, source key). The accounting state is DERIVED from the journal entry (pending, posted, reversed), never stored.

### 5a. Slice S2 is cut in two: S2a AS BUILT (cash vouchers without posting) and S2b (what remains, behind the D-214 gate)

D-214 keeps financial posting for cash and bank CLOSED until an independent accounting review and an independent security review are recorded. Slice S2 is therefore built in two cuts. **S2a creates no journal entry, allocates no number and has no ledger or balance effect**; it is the skeleton of the voucher, as 3.13 S1a was for commercial documents.

**S2a as built (company migration 0040, generated by `tools/schema/company-0040-cash-vouchers.cjs` with a `--check` CI step; no system migration).**

* **Tables.** `CashVoucher` (kind receipt or payment, cash or bank account, branch and currency of that account, date, optional party reference, reference text, note, status, idempotency key and payload hash, creator, last editor, submitter, revision), `CashVoucherLine` (counter account, positive amount, description) and `CashVoucherEvent` (append-only history: created, updated, submitted, withdrawn, cancelled). The event table belongs to the voucher (a real foreign key) instead of the shared `CashEvent` sketched in section 4; transfers get their own in S3. **Line sets are append-only:** an edit of a draft writes a whole new line set under the new revision and the voucher names its current set, so the runtime role holds SELECT and INSERT only on lines and events (never UPDATE, DELETE or TRUNCATE, section 5) and the history of a draft's lines is kept.
* **States.** draft, submitted (withdraw returns it to draft) and cancelled (from draft or submitted, with a reason; final). Nothing else is reachable: PostgreSQL refuses every other transition, including a direct statement that tries to approve or reject. The table constraints admit wider value ranges on purpose (a constraint is never dropped); the guard FUNCTIONS, which S2b replaces by a new migration, admit only the above.
* **Validation of a draft, exactly as section 6 states it (service and PostgreSQL):** an ACTIVE cash or bank account; the voucher's branch is the account's; the currency is the base currency (Q2); amounts are positive, within the range of a journal line and with no more decimals than the base currency; a counter account is postable and active, admits the base currency, is NOT bound to a cash or bank account and is NOT the control account of any party role (Q3). The same checks run again at submission, which also needs at least one line. The line's branch is the voucher's (Q11): it is not an input. At most 499 lines and a total within a journal line's range, so that S2b can always turn a voucher into one entry.
* **Idempotent create** per (creator, key): a repeated key returns the original (200, `replayed`), another payload under the same key is refused, parallel creates with one key produce one voucher.
* **Cash account master (section 4), now enforceable:** the branch and the bound ledger account are frozen once ANY voucher refers to the account, and the account is not deactivated while one of its vouchers is open (service probe and a PostgreSQL trigger on the account row). In S2a "open" is "not cancelled"; S2b refines it to "not approved, or approved with its entry neither posted nor cancelled".
* **Permissions.** `company.cashvouchers` view, create, update, submit (also withdraw) and cancel, scope Branch (the branch of the cash account; another branch answers like an unknown id). View, create, submit and cancel are ordinary. **Update is classified PRIVILEGED:** section 6 does not classify it and the pin sentence of section 8 names "update" among the privileged actions, so the fail-closed rule of D-096 applies until the lead or the owner says otherwise. The codes are in NO preset role and there is no system migration: the Company Administrator is not widened (X4-PRESET-ROLE, Option A), and a dedicated preset needs the paired trigger pattern with its own review (D-254). Until then the codes are held through roles a company defines.
* **Audit.** Business audit 2110 to 2114 (created, updated, submitted, withdrawn, cancelled) inside the block 2100-2199.
* **API (no screen: S4).** `/api/v1/company/cash-vouchers`: list, create, read, update, `submit`, `withdraw`, `cancel`. There is no delete, approve, reject, number, journal, post or reverse route and no service method for them.
* **Tests.** `CashVoucherLifecycleTests`, `CashVoucherValidationTests`, `CashVoucherIdempotencyTests`, `CashVoucherConcurrencyTests`, `CashVoucherAccessTests`, `CashVoucherGuardTests` (the guards by direct SQL), `CashVoucherFootprintTests` (the whole life of a voucher changes only the three voucher tables and the audit stream: every other table of the company database and of Hasm_System is compared, the journal and numbering tables by name), `CashVoucherRuntimeRoleTests` (the real runtime role), the matrix rows (#119, `EndpointConstraintMatrixPart2CashVoucherTests`) and the unit pins (`CashVoucherUnitTests`).

**Points S2a did NOT decide (reported to the lead; each is fail-closed or left out).**

* The classification of `company.cashvouchers.update` (privileged until decided, see above).
* A preset role for the voucher codes (none; the codes are assignable only through company-defined roles).
* Whether an INACTIVE party may be referenced: the plan only says the party is an optional reference, so S2a requires that it exists and nothing more.
* The base-currency lock: a voucher of S2a is not registered as financial history (nothing is approved or posted), so it does not lock the base currency by itself; PostgreSQL refuses to write or submit a voucher whose currency is not the base currency. S2b posts through the journal, whose probe locks.
* Withdrawal (submitted back to draft) is not named in the state machine of section 6; it was built on the lead's instruction for this slice, as in 3.13 S1a, with the submit permission.

**S2b (remaining part of slice S2; needs the D-214 reviews before any of it is enabled).** Independent-approver approval bound to the revision (D-212; the approver is never the creator, the last editor or the submitter: the three are recorded by S2a) and rejection with a reason; the permission codes `approve` and `reverse` and the Cash Approver preset (a sensitive grant); the official number allocated at approval and never reused; the fiscal-period check at approval and at posting (it is deliberately NOT a rule of a draft); the journal entry created in the approval transaction under the source key `cashvoucher:<id>`, the link row and the DERIVED accounting state; the journal module's refusal to cancel or edit a voucher-owned entry; the privileged `reverse` action; the optional cost-center allocation of a line "as the journal supports it"; the extension of the approval-policy engine (D-212) and the no-negative-cashbox rule (D-213) where they apply; the deactivation rule of D-259 (non-zero balance, unsettled documents) before posting is activated; and a re-check of the counter-account rules at approval (S2a checks them when a line is written and at submission; an account can change afterwards). ## 6. State machines, rules and permissions

* **Voucher or transfer:** draft -> submitted -> approved; rejected returns to draft with a reason; cancel only before approval (a number is allocated at approval, so a cancelled draft never consumed one). **After approval the document is IMMUTABLE: it cannot be edited and it is never approved a second time**, so the journal entry created at approval is the only entry the voucher ever owns (see below); its journal entry is pending or posted through the journal workflow (Q4). The only ways out of "approved" are the posting of that entry, or the privileged voucher-level `reverse` action (next bullet).
* **Void and reversal only through the voucher (fixes the "cancelled draft entry" gap):** the draft journal entry of an approved voucher or transfer can be cancelled ONLY through a voucher action: the privileged `reverse` permission, audited with a reason, which VOIDS the voucher (the official number stays void and is never reused) and cancels its pending draft entry in the same transaction. For a POSTED entry the same action creates the reversal entry (existing `ReverseAsync`) and marks the voucher reversed. The journal module refuses (service check plus a database guard, stable refusal code) to cancel or edit an entry whose source key belongs to a voucher or transfer. Consequently a voucher has AT MOST ONE non-cancelled entry over its whole life and nothing can re-create an entry after a cancellation.
* **Approval rules:** an approver is never the creator, the last editor or the submitter; approval is bound to the exact revision (If-Match); approval is final (an approved voucher is not edited, so SRS 4's rule that a material edit sends an approved-but-not-posted document back to approval has nothing to return: this is consistent with Q9 and keeps one entry per voucher; an owner may later define a return-to-approval sequence, which would be a new decision with its own tests).
* **Dates:** the voucher date must fall in an Open fiscal period (a Soft Closed period needs the existing soft-closed posting permission; Final Closed is refused), checked by `FiscalPeriodGate` at approval and again when the journal posts; the date of the operation and the approval date are both recorded (SRS 9 last bullet, by analogy).
* **Counter accounts (fail closed until Q3 and 3.17 decide):** a counter account must be postable and active, its currency policy must be compatible with the base currency (Base, Any, or Specific equal to the base currency), it must NOT be an account bound to any cash or bank account (that would be a transfer in disguise and would bypass the Q7 checks) and it must NOT be configured as the control account of any party role (3.9B; unallocated postings to receivable or payable control accounts are an accounting treatment Q3 leaves to the owner). **Each voucher line's branch equals the voucher's branch** (the cash account's) until 3.22 defines inter-branch settlement (Q11); cost-center allocation stays as the journal supports it.
* **Branch authorization:** a voucher belongs to the branch of its cash or bank account; a user acts only on branches they hold; the transfer needs the permission for the branch of BOTH accounts.
* **Permissions (names confirmed by the lead):** `company.cashaccounts` (scope Branch: view, create, update, activate), `company.cashaccounts.viewsensitive` (see below), `company.cashvouchers` (scope Branch: view, create, update, submit, approve, cancel, reverse), `company.cashtransfers` (scope Branch: the same actions), and reuse of `company.ledgerreports` for RR-4.
* **Classification under D-096 (central `PrivilegedAccess`, fail closed; slice S1 adds it with pin tests, and a resource added without a classification already fails the completeness test):**
  * **Privileged:** `approve` and `reverse` of vouchers and transfers (approve is already privileged by action; `reverse` is added to `PrivilegedPermissions`), `company.cashaccounts.update` and `company.cashaccounts.activate` (changing the bound GL account or the bank details is a payment-redirection vector), and `viewsensitive`. A role holding any of these is privileged: MFA is mandatory for it (D-096, D-080).
  * **Ordinary:** `view`, `create`, `submit` and `cancel` of vouchers and transfers, and `view` of accounts. The three resources are listed in `OrdinaryResources`; the privileged permissions above are listed individually.
  * **Naming note:** the code `company.cashaccounts.viewsensitive` needs either a new permission action `ViewSensitive` (which must then be added to the privileged actions) or the precedent of a separate resource (as `company.attachmentssensitive`); the implementation PR chooses, keeps the lead's code name, and pins the result.
  * **Presets:** a **Cash Approver** preset (approve and reverse, plus view) is a SENSITIVE grant: through the administrative tool now (D-099), through the 4.1 `role.grant.sensitive` framework later (D-105); the Company Administrator preset does NOT contain approve, reverse or viewsensitive.
* **Audit:** a NEW contiguous business-audit block is reserved at S1 (proposal 2100-2199; the lead confirms, because 2000-2029 are allocated to retention and backup). Events: account created or updated or deactivated; voucher created, submitted, approved, rejected, cancelled, journal created, reversed; transfer likewise.
* **Bank-detail changes:** every change of the bank name, bank branch or account number is audited with the MASKED old and new values only (never the full number, in the audit detail or in logs) and raises a notification through the D-102 route (an audit event plus the in-app administrator list; the outbound channel as configured).
* **S1 implementation notes (security review of PR 206; technical rules, not business rules).** (1) **Masking:** the mask reveals no more than a fixed safe share of an accepted number: from 12 characters the last four, from 8 to 11 the last two, below 8 nothing (`****`); the accepted length (4 to 34 letters or digits) is unchanged. Only that tail is stored beside the encrypted number and it is the only part any reader, audit event, report or export may show. (2) **Key lifecycle:** the key ids of the encrypted numbers are part of the company key inventory of every backup (they are read from the company database and added to the attachment key ids, so an inventory is never complete while bank numbers need other keys), and `POST /api/v1/company/cash-accounts/maintenance/rewrap` re-wraps the numbers under the current master key (company-wide privileged update; idempotent; fail-closed; audit 2106 and 2107). `CashAccountService.KeyUsageAsync` gives the per-key count for the key retirement report.

## 9. Risks and how the plan contains them

| Risk | Containment |
|---|---|
| A duplicate receipt or entry on retry or concurrency | source key unique among non-cancelled entries, row-locked approval, create idempotency key, concurrency tests |
| A voucher posted into a closed period | `FiscalPeriodGate` at approval and the journal's own database guard at posting; period tests |
| Cash balance drifts from the ledger | no stored balance; the report reads the ledger; reconciliation test |
| Unauthorized branch access | branch scope on every route, matrix rows, branch isolation tests |
| A hidden accounting rule for unallocated money | Q3 default is explicit counter accounts only; nothing is inferred from a party |
| Approval bypass by automation | the automatic path never skips the approval gate; SoD in service and database |

## 10. What is explicitly NOT assumed

* No default chart accounts, no seeded counter accounts and no automatic receivable, payable or advance posting.
* No minimum balance, no cross-branch or cross-currency transfer, no FX difference, no cheque, no bank statement matching and no invoice allocation in this phase.
* No new approval-policy semantics: S5a (#136) is a precedent, not a dependency; if the owner wants policy-engine coverage, that is a separate extension.
* No change to journals, stock documents, commercial documents or any merged table.
