# Phase 3.2C — General Ledger completion

**Status:** in progress on `feature/phase-3-2c-gl-completion` (from main after PR #17). Nothing is applied to any permanent database. Company migration `0009_company_gl_completion.sql` and system migration `0012_cost_center_scope.sql` are built slice by slice before the phase is merged.

## Owner-approved scope
Slices **S1** cost-center accounting policies, **S2** risk-based and ordered approval policies, **S3** fiscal-year closing and opening balances. Everything else stays on the roadmap (3.2D generic import framework, 3.2A-2 chart templates, 3.2A-3 Excel chart import, 3.3 Excel/PDF exports and financial statements, realized/unrealized FX differences with receivables/payables/banking, and the remaining modules). None of those is implemented here.

## S1 — cost-center accounting policies (as built)

### Approved decisions and how they are enforced
| Decision | Enforcement |
|---|---|
| Cost centers are optional by default | No rule = no requirement; nothing changes for existing companies |
| A company may require a cost center for particular accounts | `AccountCostCenterRule` (leaf accounts only, revisioned, audited, never deleted). **PostgreSQL** refuses submission and posting of an entry with a line on a requiring account that has no allocation (trigger on the entry status change, so no writer can bypass it). Reversals and the system rounding line are exempt: a reversal mirrors an earlier posting and must stay possible after a policy was introduced |
| Lines may be split across several cost centers, reconciling exactly | Existing deferred PostgreSQL check (allocations sum exactly to the line in line currency and base currency) |
| Deterministic rounding | New percentage mode: `AllocationMath` (largest-remainder on exact decimals; ties by larger percentage then lower cost-center id) so equal inputs always give equal amounts, whatever the entry order; percentages must total exactly 100 (max 4 decimals), cannot be mixed with amounts, and a share rounding to zero is refused |
| Cost centers independent of branches; optional eligibility restriction | `CostCenterBranchRule` + `CostCenterBranchEligibility` (rows toggled, never deleted). When restricted, PostgreSQL refuses submission/posting of an allocation to the cost center for a line whose branch is not eligible. Unrestricted by default |
| Cost-center-scoped permissions | New `ScopeKind.CostCenter`. The scope check of the main assignment table cannot be extended without a DROP (forbidden), so cost-center assignments live in their own table (system migration 0012: epoch-bound, soft-revoked only, never deleted). A grant covers the cost center and its descendants and is effective only while the cost center is active with the activation epoch it was granted under (the epoch is maintained by PostgreSQL and bumped on reactivation, like branches and warehouses). Only `company.journal.*` and `company.ledgerreports.*` accept this scope; every other permission stays inert under it |
| One authorization rule for listing, details, operations and reports | An entry is covered by a permission if (1) it is held company-wide, or (2) BRANCH path: held for the entry branch and every line branch, or (3) COST-CENTER path: held at cost-center scope, every line is allocated and ALL allocations lie inside the granted subtrees. The paths are alternatives (union): a branch grant is never narrowed by a cost-center grant, and company-wide access stays distinct. The same rule runs in the list query (inside SQL, before ORDER/LIMIT/OFFSET), in every operation (create, update, submit, post, reverse, approve) and in the adjusting-entry reference check |
| Unauthorized cost-center data is not exposed | Reports restrict INSIDE the query: lines of permitted branches count in full; every other line contributes only its allocation shares inside the granted cost centers, in base AND original currency (never the full line or another center's share). Explicit filters outside the grant return nothing. The journal editor's cost-center list is a separate lookup (`/api/v1/company/journal/cost-centers`) limited to the caller's grants, independent of the administration permission |
| Hierarchical reporting without double counting | Only leaves receive allocations; groups roll up their leaves once; a filter on a group includes its descendants; a viewer granted a group sees each leaf share once. Tested including a branch grant combined with a cost-center grant |
| Posted allocations stay historically correct | Allocations store the stable cost-center id and amounts; re-coding, moving or deactivating a cost center never rewrites them. Group roll-ups follow the CURRENT tree (a moved leaf feeds its new parent from then on, for all dates), which is the standard reading of a master-data hierarchy; the leaf figures never change |

### Migrations and permissions
* Company migration `0009` (S1 part): `PolicyHistory` (append-only history of the 3.2C policy entities; the 3.2B ledger history table restricts its entity codes and cannot be altered without a DROP), `AccountCostCenterRule`, `CostCenterBranchRule`, `CostCenterBranchEligibility`, the cost-center activation epoch column with its database-maintained trigger, and the submission/posting policy trigger. Runtime privileges: SELECT/INSERT/UPDATE on the three rule tables, SELECT/INSERT on the history; no DELETE anywhere new.
* System migration `0012`: `CostCenterAssignment` table + immutability trigger; permissions `company.costcenterpolicies.view|configure` (company-wide) added to the preset Company Administrator. Cost-center scoping needs no new permission code.
* API: `/api/v1/company/cost-center-policies/accounts[/{id}]`, `/branches[/{id}]` (If-Match with the rule revision, 0 = never configured), `/api/v1/company/journal/cost-centers` (lookup); role assignment accepts scope `costcenter`.
* Audit events 1061 (account rule changed), 1062 (branch eligibility changed).

### Limitations and deferred decisions (not invented)
* A cost-center-scoped user must have EVERY line of an entry allocated inside the grant to see or act on it (no line-by-line partial access to an entry). Reports, being aggregates, do show the permitted shares of mixed entries.
* There is no in-app screen for assigning roles at cost-center scope yet (the role-assignment API supports it; the assignment administration UI does not exist for any scope in this release line).
* The cost-center rule applies to postable (non-group) accounts; a later change of an account to a group is possible only while the account has no postings and leaves a dormant rule that never matches a line.

## S2 — risk-based and ordered approval policies (as built)

### Model
* A policy may carry **risk conditions** (each optional, empty = do not care): journal type (normal / reversal / adjusting), a **manual exchange-rate override** on any line, the entry date lying in a **Soft Closed period** right now, the year-end class (closing / opening-balance entries and their reversals), together with the existing document type, source, branch and base-currency threshold. The "privileged exceptions" the owner listed are exactly the manual rate override and soft-closed posting; other risk classifications need an owner decision and are not invented.
* A policy may be **staged**: an ordered list of stages (no business cap on the number of levels; each stage needs 1–1000 distinct approvers and may be bound to a role). A policy without stages is the **classic policy of 3.2B**: one stage of N distinct approvers.
* **Selection is deterministic**: among the active policies whose conditions match the entry, the most demanding wins (most approvals in total, then most stages, then the oldest policy, then the lowest id). One SQL function (`pick`) decides, and the service and the database gate use the same one.
* **Binding snapshot (a floor)**: submitting an entry snapshots the plan it was submitted under for that revision (append-only). The effective plan is the governing policy right now, raised per stage to that snapshot: a policy changed later can ADD requirements for a pending document (tightening is honoured at approval and posting) but can never silently remove what it was submitted under (deactivating or loosening a policy does not release pending documents). A new revision (edit, withdrawal, rejection) is bound again at its next submission.

### Enforcement (PostgreSQL, for every writer)
* **Ordering**: a decision for a journal entry's current revision must be cast for the first stage that is not complete (trigger on the decision table); a later stage waits, a complete stage takes no more decisions. Distinctness spans the whole chain (one decision per approver per revision).
* **Gate**: the entry cannot become Approved or Posted unless every stage of the effective plan has its approvers and nobody rejected the revision (trigger on the entry); posting re-evaluates it. Manual and automatic entries go through the same trigger.
* **Revision binding, stale approvals and separation of duties**: approvals belong to the exact revision; creator and submitter can never approve (service), and the approval permission is re-read on every decision, so a revoked approver is refused before anything is recorded. A stage bound to a role additionally needs the approver to hold that role at that moment (the service refuses; without role information the stage fails closed). The role is validated when the policy is configured (it must exist, be active and hold the approval permission).
* **Policy history and audit**: every create/update/activate keeps its stage set per revision (append-only versions; toggling copies the set), conditions and stages are part of the audited change record, and stages and bindings are append-only tables (no UPDATE or DELETE for the runtime role).

### Migration of existing policies (safe and explicit)
Nothing is converted. A classic policy keeps its exact meaning (one stage, N distinct approvers, no conditions): it is evaluated by the same SQL function as before and appears in the new model as a single implicit stage, so no configuration is ambiguous and none needs an explicit resolution. Editing a classic policy into stages is an ordinary audited revision.

### Limitations
* Role eligibility is verified by the service against the authorization store (a different database); PostgreSQL enforces counts, order, distinctness and revision, not role membership (the documented trade-off of every cross-database check).
* The UI edits stage counts and conditions; binding a stage to a role is available through the API (there is no role-administration UI in this release line).

## S3 — fiscal-year closing and opening balances (as built)

### Architectural checkpoint (verified before any irreversible logic)
1. **Immutable posted journals and FiscalPeriodGate**: closing and opening entries are ordinary journal entries (drafted by the service, then approved and posted through the normal workflow), so numbering, approvals, period checks and atomic posting are never bypassed. The closing entry is dated the **last day** of the year (the last period must be Open or Soft Closed, never Final Closed: the preview reports it and refuses); the opening entry is dated the **first day** of the next year. **No 13th period and no new period type** were needed.
2. **Two-person reopening**: a Final Closed last period is reopened only through the existing approval workflow; nothing here bypasses it. A reopened year may receive postings; the closing then becomes **stale** (a live comparison with the ledger) and a new period-close probe (`year_end_closing`) refuses to close a period of a year whose posted closing or carried balances no longer match. The correction is explicit and auditable: **reverse the opening balances, reverse the closing, close again as version n+1, carry forward again** (PostgreSQL refuses to post the closing reversal before the opening balances are reversed, and refuses a new version while any earlier one is active).
3. **Multiple open years and next-year activity**: the opening entry is an additional entry in the next year (nothing existing is overwritten); balance-sheet reporting is year-wise from the latest year that received posted opening balances (the opening entry replaces everything earlier, so carried balances are never counted twice and reports of earlier years never change); a year can be closed only after every earlier year with profit-and-loss activity has a posted, current closing.
4. **Historical report correctness**: reports accept `excludeClosing` to show a year's activity without the closing entries; with closing entries included every profit-and-loss balance of a closed year is zero and equity holds the result.
5. **The existing close checks blocked ALL closes** once any entry was posted (the 3.1D fail-closed rule met the unimplemented `pending_adjustments` and `inventory_valuation_differences` domains). Resolved without weakening it: `pending_adjustments` now has a real probe (pending reversal/adjusting entries), `year_end_closing` is a real probe, and `inventory_valuation_differences` belongs to a module that does not exist in this release, so it is listed as **not applicable** (never "passed", never "implemented") and does not make closing refuse; the closing validations, the fiscal API and the history of every close say so. The module that introduces such data must replace it with a real probe (the schema-contract test lists new tables deliberately).

### Functionality
* **Settings**: the company chooses the equity account that receives the annual result (never hard-coded): an active, postable Equity account of the company (service and PostgreSQL), plus "always require an independent approval for year-end entries" (default on, audited, revisioned).
* **Preview / dry run** (read-only, one consistent snapshot): net result, the exact closing lines (per profit-and-loss account and branch, the result per branch on the equity account), the opening lines (balance-sheet balances per account and branch, base currency), a reproducible SHA-256 evidence hash and the validations: settings, earlier years closed, no pending entries in the year, last period accepts the entry, accounts with balances active, no accounts tied to a foreign currency, no foreign-currency balance-sheet positions, entry size, no active closing, next year exists with an open first period, closing posted and current, not already carried forward, plus the domain list above.
* **Start closing / carry forward**: user-initiated, separate permissions (`company.fiscalclosing.create` plus company-wide `company.journal.create`), serialized by an advisory lock, atomic with the closing record and the audit event. Entries get source keys (`yearclose:{year}:{version}`, `yearopen:{year}:{n}`), so a retry never duplicates. The initiator is the creator and submitter and can never approve; at least one independent approval is always required unless the company switched the setting off, and approval policies can target year-end entries (conditions `closing` / `opening`) with ordered stages.
* **PostgreSQL integrity** for every writer: one active closing per year, one active carryforward per year, closing dated the last day, opening dated the first day of the directly following year, a closing entry posts only while it matches the ledger exactly (every profit-and-loss balance inverted per account and branch, the result per branch on the chosen account, nothing else), an opening entry only after its closing is posted and while it matches the balance sheet at the end of the closed year, stale drafts must be regenerated, records are append-only.
* **Carryforward without a closing** is allowed only for a year with no profit-and-loss activity (PostgreSQL refuses it otherwise).
* **Reversals** are drafted through the journal engine (`reverse-opening`, `reverse-closing`, optional date) and follow the approval and posting rules; permissions `company.fiscalclosing.reverse` plus journal reverse.
* **UI**: one shared screen (Company, Fiscal-year closing) with settings, year selector, preview with validations (not applicable shown as such), the entries that will be generated, the closings with their state (prepared, closed, opening prepared, carried forward, reversed), staleness and the reversal actions. Reports have an "exclude closing entries" option. Approval policies have the year-end condition.

### Migrations, permissions, audit
* Company migration `0009` (S2/S3 part): `ApprovalPolicyStage`, `DocumentApprovalBinding`, the decision stage column, policy condition columns, `ClosingSettings`, `FiscalYearClosing`, `FiscalYearCarryforward`, the functions pick / plan / satisfied / kind / pnl / floor / match closing / match opening / stale, and the triggers (stage order, approval gate, year-end posting integrity, insert guards, settings account validation). The classic `required` function is replaced (same signature) to cover classic policies only.
* System migration `0012` (S3 part): `company.fiscalclosing.view|configure|create|reverse` (company-wide) for the preset Company Administrator.
* Audit events 1063 (settings), 1064 (closing generated), 1065 (opening balances generated); policy history entities 4 (closing settings) and 5 (year-end processing).

### Known limitations (not invented; owner decisions needed)
* **Foreign-currency balances** (balance-sheet accounts holding a non-zero position in a foreign currency, or accounts tied to one foreign currency) block the closing or the carryforward: carrying them needs the foreign-exchange revaluation policy of the receivables, payables and banking phases (realized and unrealized FX differences stay on the roadmap).
* Cost centers and other analytic dimensions are not carried into the opening balances (the branch is); cost centers on balance-sheet accounts have no approved carry semantics.
* An entry is limited to 500 lines (the journal limit): a year with more than 500 (account, branch) balances cannot be carried in one entry (the preview reports it).
* Balance-sheet accounts in the trial balance and general ledger are reported year-wise from the latest carried year: a range that spans a carried year shows that year's balance-sheet activity from its opening entry. `excludeClosing` is meant for views that start at the year start.
