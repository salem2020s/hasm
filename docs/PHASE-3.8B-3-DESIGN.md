# Phase 3.8B-3 — Negative-stock exception: technical and accounting design

Status: **DESIGN FOR INDEPENDENT REVIEW — nothing is implemented.** Basis: the owner policy for N1–N4 (recorded in the owner message of 2026-11-16), SRS §9 (negative stock forbidden by default; limited authorized exception for eligible moving-average items with a provisional cost and later settlement; never FIFO, lot or serial; the period-close rule for material pending differences; retroactive entries never edit posted history), PHASE-3.6 (costing engine), PHASE-3.7A–D (accounting configuration, posting, reversal, close probe), ARCHITECTURE §7–§8.

## 1. Core decision: do not weaken the engine, model the obligation beside it
The costing position of an item and warehouse keeps every existing CHECK (never a negative quantity or value, no value without quantity, no negative FIFO layer). **No existing constraint is relaxed.** A negative issue is split:
* the **covered part** (up to the quantity on hand) goes through the engine exactly as today at the real moving-average cost;
* the **uncovered part** Qn never touches the position. It becomes a **provisional obligation**: a separate, narrowly constrained record that says "Qn units left the warehouse before they were received, valued provisionally at p per unit".
So the position stays a true physical stock that can never be negative, the weighted average and FIFO layers cannot be corrupted by the provisional figure, and the exception cannot leak into ordinary stock. (The physical stock is then shown by the balances screen as quantity on hand MINUS the open obligation quantity (shown as a separate column; see revision 2, R3).)

## 2. Eligibility and authorization (N4)
* Default stays prohibited. Three independent switches must all be on: **company enablement** (a versioned, audited company setting, off by default), **item eligibility** (an explicit per-item flag, settable only for items whose resolved method is moving weighted average and that are neither lot nor serial tracked; the database refuses the flag on any other item, and the method of a position is fixed at its first entry, so a later method change cannot make a flagged item FIFO silently: changing the method of an item with an open obligation or the flag is refused), and a **dedicated warehouse-scoped permission** `company.stockdocs.negativeexception` held by the user who approves the exception.
* **Approval control and segregation of duties:** an issue that would exceed stock is refused by default. When the exception applies, the document needs an explicit *exception approval* (a separate, audited event recorded on the document with the approver, time and reason) by a user holding the permission who is **not** the poster and not the document creator (configurable: the company may require a second distinct approver). Posting without the recorded exception approval is refused; no background or automatic process creates or consumes an exception approval.
* Transfers may **not** use the exception (a transfer carries the actual cost to the receiving warehouse; a provisional cost must never travel): refused.

## 3. Provisional cost (N1)
Resolved at posting, in this priority, and stored immutably on the obligation with source, user, time and references:
1. the **last valid moving weighted-average unit cost** of that item and warehouse. The engine keeps `last_average_unit_cost` per position, written whenever the position holds quantity and **retained when it falls to zero** (today the average is only derivable while quantity exists); source code `last_average`;
2. else the **latest reliable receipt unit cost** (the most recent non-reversed receipt entry of that item and warehouse with quantity and value; source `last_receipt`, reference = the entry);
3. else an **explicitly authorized manual provisional unit cost**: entered by the exception approver with a mandatory justification (source `manual`, justification stored, audited). A zero or missing cost is **never** assumed: with no source and no manual cost the issue is refused (`provisional_cost_unavailable`).
The chosen cost is part of the approval snapshot, so what the approver saw is what posts (a stale snapshot is refused and must be re-approved).

## 4. Data model (company migration, additive)
* `StockNegativeObligation` (append-only except the single open-quantity counter, which only decreases, enforced by trigger): id, item, warehouse, originating stock document and posting and cost-operation references, issuing journal entry, original quantity (base unit, > 0), open quantity (0 to original), provisional unit cost (> 0), provisional total, source code, justification, created by, time, approval reference, status (open, settled, reversed).
* `StockNegativeSettlement` (append-only): obligation, the receipt posting it was matched to, matched quantity (> 0), provisional unit cost, actual unit cost, difference amount (signed, exact), settlement journal entry, effective date, time and user; `unique(obligation, receipt entry)`; the matched quantity of an obligation never exceeds its original; the matched quantity of a receipt line never exceeds the receipt quantity (database triggers).
* `InventoryAccountingVersion` gains two **new configured accounts** (a new immutable version, nothing hard-coded or seeded): the **provisional obligation account** (a liability-type, postable account) and the **settlement variance account** (default: the original issue's counter account, i.e. cost of sales; an explicit variance account may be set). The existing inventory and counter mappings are untouched. Unconfigured company: the exception is refused.
* Position: `last_average_unit_cost`, written by the engine only.
Concurrency: every operation takes the same per-item-and-warehouse advisory lock and document lock as posting (3.7B); matching is serialized per item and warehouse.

## 5. Accounting (double entry, base currency, exact decimal arithmetic)
Let I = inventory account (resolved for the item group), K = issue counter account (cost of sales), G = receipt counter account (goods received), O = provisional obligation account, V = variance account (default K).

**5.1 Issue of 10 units when 3 are on hand at average 5.00 (provisional cost p = 5.00 from last average).**
* Covered part: 3 units × 5.00 = 15.00 through the engine: Dr K 15.00 / Cr I 15.00.
* Uncovered part: 7 units × 5.00 = 35.00 as an obligation: Dr K 35.00 / Cr O 35.00.
* One balanced journal entry (accounting state as today: draft pending, or no journal when zero value). Inventory is 0 quantity / 0 value; the open obligation is 7 at 5.00.

**5.2 Full settlement by a receipt of 7 at 6.00.**
* Matched 7: provisional 35.00, actual 42.00, difference 7.00 (actual above provisional).
* Dr O 35.00, Dr V 7.00 / Cr G 42.00. Nothing enters inventory (the goods were already issued). The position is unchanged.
* If the actual cost were 4.00 (actual 28.00, difference -7.00): Dr O 35.00 / Cr V 7.00, Cr G 28.00.

**5.3 Partial receipt, then another.** Obligation 7 at 5.00. Receipt 1: 4 at 6.00 → matched 4: Dr O 20.00, Dr V 4.00 / Cr G 24.00; open 3. Receipt 2: 10 at 4.50 → matched 3 (provisional 15.00, actual 13.50, difference -1.50): Dr O 15.00 / Cr V 1.50, Cr G 13.50; the remaining 7 units enter inventory normally at 4.50: Dr I 31.50 / Cr G 31.50 (the engine receives 7 at an exact amount of 31.50). The weighted average therefore never sees the provisional cost.

**5.4 Several obligations.** Matching is deterministic: obligations of the item and warehouse are consumed **oldest first** by (effective date, creation sequence); within one receipt the matched split is stored per obligation. A receipt line is matched before any quantity enters the position.

**5.5 Reversal.** Reversing a receipt that settled obligations re-opens the settled quantities exactly (compensating settlement entries: the settlement journal is reversed through the existing reversal machinery, the obligation's open quantity is restored by a new append-only row, never an update of history). Reversing the negative issue is refused while any settlement exists; settlements must be reversed first (newest first). Reversal after partial settlement restores exactly the unsettled and settled parts in order.

**5.6 Settlement spanning fiscal periods.** A settlement is dated at its receipt's effective date and posted in that period. The original issue stays in its own period, never edited. If the receipt's period is soft-closed the existing posting authorization rules apply; if its period is **Final Closed** the receipt (and therefore the settlement) cannot be posted at all: the receipt is blocked with the unresolved obligation reported (`period_closed`), never backdated. A receipt dated in an open period settles obligations from earlier (including closed) periods; the variance lands in the receipt's open period. The "approved current-period correction workflow" named in the owner policy **does not exist in the system today**; this design therefore blocks and reports rather than inventing one (see C3).

## 6. Period close and reconciliation (N3)
* The existing `inventory_valuation_differences` close probe is extended: **any open obligation with a document date on or before the period end blocks Final Close** (and is reported at soft close as a warning on the closing checklist, as the SRS says only material pending differences block). The blocking report lists, per obligation: item, warehouse, originating document, open quantity, provisional value, age, and what resolves it (a receipt to match, or the reversal of the issue).
* Reconciliation: for each period end, `costing position value = inventory GL balance` stays as 3.7C verifies; a new line checks **open obligation provisional total = GL balance of O**, and **sum of settlement differences = the postings to V attributable to settlements**. Any mismatch blocks the close with the failing figure.
* A settlement journal in a pending or cancelled state counts as a posting problem exactly like 3.7C states (derived from facts).

## 7. Database-enforced invariants (narrow)
Obligation: quantity > 0, open between 0 and original, unit cost > 0, source in {1,2,3}, manual source requires a justification, the item must be flagged eligible and its position method moving average (trigger reads the position's immutable method), no update except decreasing open quantity or the status change, no delete or truncate. Settlement: append-only, positive matched quantity, cumulative matched ≤ original, difference = exact `matched × (actual − provisional)` recomputed by a trigger. Item flag: refused unless moving-average and untracked; refused to unset or change method while an open obligation exists. None of the existing position or layer constraints changes.

## 8. Interactions and conflicts to be decided in review
* **C1 (SRS silent):** two new accounts (obligation, variance) extend the inventory accounting configuration; the SRS names neither. Proposed as configuration, no default.
* **C2:** `last_average_unit_cost` is a new engine-maintained column; the engine's existing invariants and the 3.6 reconciliation must include it (it must equal the last average the position had while quantity existed).
* **C3 (owner policy item without a mechanism):** "approved current-period correction workflow" does not exist; blocking and reporting is proposed. If the owner wants such a workflow it is a separate phase.
* **C4 (3.8D):** back-dated movements and recalculation must treat obligations (a back-dated receipt may settle an older obligation, a back-dated issue may create one). 3.8D must be designed against this document; until then back-dated movements that touch an item with an open obligation are refused.
* **C5 (exception and approval):** a second distinct approver is configurable; the default requires the approver to differ from the poster.
* **C6 (transfers):** excluded by design (provisional cost must not move).

## 9. Test plan (PostgreSQL, disposable cluster only)
Provisional-cost priority and manual cost with justification and refusal when none; refusal for FIFO, lot, serial, unflagged items, a company without enablement, a user without the permission, the poster approving their own exception; split of a mixed issue (covered plus uncovered) with exact figures; full, partial and multiple settlements with positive and negative differences and journals balancing to the cent; oldest-first determinism; reversal restoring exact open quantities (with settlement reversal order); settlement across fiscal periods and Final Close blocking; probe and reconciliation figures; exactly-once under concurrent postings and concurrent receipts; every database invariant attacked directly; append-only trigger tests; weighted average unchanged by obligations; no existing test or constraint changed.

## Revision 2 (after the independent review of e11277c; this section overrides sections 3 to 9 wherever they differ)
Review result: no Critical; High H1 to H6 and Medium M1 to M10 are resolved in this revision. A short re-review is required before any code.

**R1 (H1, schema).** A fully uncovered issue and a fully matched receipt produce no costing entry, but `StockPosting.cost_operation` is NOT NULL and unique and a costing operation needs entries. This is an **explicit schema change to existing tables and is NOT assumed approved**. Proposal for the owner: a costing operation of a new kind "provisional" is allowed with zero entries, `StockPosting.cost_operation` stays NOT NULL (no 0016 column change), and the operation row carries the obligation or settlement reference. Alternative: a nullable `cost_operation` guarded by a CHECK that a negative-exception record exists. Implementation of 3.8B-3 waits for the owner's approval of one of the two.

**R2 (H2, rounding).** No unit cost is stored or used to compute an amount. Stored per obligation: original quantity (base unit) and the **provisional total** (rounded once, as in 3.6). The provisional unit cost shown to people is derived. Each match allocates provisional and actual amounts with the engine's proportional rule (`amount * part / whole`, the exact remainder on the LAST part: the final match of an obligation takes the provisional remainder; the unmatched remainder of a receipt line takes the receipt remainder). Triggers check: matched provisional + open provisional = original total, and per receipt line matched actual + unmatched amount = line amount. Source 1 stores the last average as a (value, quantity) pair, not a unit cost. A provisional total that rounds to zero is refused (`provisional_cost_unavailable`), never accepted as zero.

**R3 (H3, inflows).** Invariant: **an open obligation implies the position quantity is 0** (trigger and test). Receipts match first (oldest obligation first, only obligations dated on or before the receipt's effective date), then the rest enters the position. Every other inflow into that item and warehouse is **refused while an obligation is open** (transfer-in, reversal of a covered issue, stocktake surplus of 3.8B-2, opening stock of 3.27) with `negative_obligation_open`. This is the conservative proposal and is an accounting choice listed for the owner.

**R4 (H4, further double-entry examples; I, K, G, O, V as in section 5).**
* Transfer: a transfer OUT never uses the exception (refused). A transfer IN into a warehouse holding an obligation is refused (R3): obligation 7 at 5.00 in warehouse B, transfer 7 units from A to B at actual cost 6.00 is refused until a receipt settles the obligation. After the settlement of 5.2 the same transfer is accepted: Dr I(B) 42.00 / Cr I(A) 42.00.
* Reversal of the settling receipt (5.2 reversed): the settlement journal is reversed: Cr O 35.00, Cr V 7.00 / Dr G 42.00 (the exact opposite of 5.2) and the obligation is open again with 7 units and provisional 35.00. Reversal of a partial settlement reverses only that settlement (newest first).
* Settlement across fiscal periods: issue of 7 units on 28 Dec year 1: Dr K 35.00 / Cr O 35.00 in year 1. The open obligation blocks Final Close of year 1 (N3), so year 1 can only be open or soft-closed. A receipt of 7 at 6.00 dated 3 Jan year 2 (open period) posts in year 2: Dr O 35.00, Dr V 7.00 / Cr G 42.00. At 31 Dec year 1 the point-in-time balance of O is 35.00 and it is cleared in year 2. The issue period is never edited.
* All amounts are base currency; the receipt amount is the base-currency amount already stored.

**R5 (H5, mutability).** One model: obligation rows are **immutable**; open quantity and open provisional are **derived** as original minus the net of the settlement rows, where a settlement reversal is its own append-only row with negative matched quantity and negative amounts (`reverses_settlement_id`, unique). Constraints are on the net: cumulative net matched between 0 and original, net matched provisional between 0 and the original total. Unique key: (obligation, receipt posting line, reverses_settlement_id nullable).

**R6 (H6, eligibility and method).** The method truth is the position's immutable method. The first obligation creates the position (sequence 0, method fixed at that moment from the policy chain, which must resolve to moving average) in the same transaction. The item flag is enforced by the service as the authority and by the database as a backstop (a trigger on the obligation insert checks the flag, the position method and tracking = none at that moment). Guard triggers refuse changing the item flag or tracking, the item's group, a group method policy, or the company default method when that would change the resolved method of an item with an open obligation (the exact list of guarded tables is a re-review item).

**R7 (M1, locks).** Exception approval, the item flag, enablement, matching, settlement reversal and item or warehouse deactivation take the company-wide warehouse-structure advisory lock first, then the document row lock, then the position row and the obligation rows `FOR UPDATE` in (effective date, sequence) order, exactly as 3.7B and 3.7C. Trigger guards do not rely on `SUM` under READ COMMITTED alone: they run after the rows are locked.

**R8 (M2, configuration drift).** O, V and the counter account come from the configuration version recorded on the ISSUE posting and from the counter account actually credited at issue (an adjustment issue of 3.8B-2 uses the adjustment counter account, not K). Save-time validation: O and V active, postable, not foreign-currency-only, never an inventory account, O different from K. A reconfiguration never strands a balance.

**R9 (M3 to M10, L3).**
* A receipt in a soft-closed period is refused (existing `allowSoftClosed = false`); section 5.6 is corrected accordingly. Final Closed is never backdated.
* Reconciliation is point-in-time (settlement history up to the date; the GL side counts posted entries). An obligation whose issue journal is pending is reported as pending; one whose journal is cancelled or reversed refuses settlement (`issue_accounting_failed`). Account O accepts automatic postings only (manual entries refused), and each settlement stores its journal link so "postings to V from settlements" is a stored sum.
* The settlement journal is created in the same transaction as the receipt posting, idempotency key `stock_negative_settlement:<obligation>:<receipt posting line>`; a receipt reversal reverses its settlement journals under the 3.7C rule for unposted entries (the reversal stays in the posting's period). A receipt reversal that would re-open an obligation whose issue period is Final Closed is refused.
* A receipt dated before the obligation does not match.
* Deactivating an item or warehouse with an open obligation is refused. Turning company enablement off or revoking the permission never blocks settlement (no exception approval is needed to settle). Obligations and matching are in the base unit; alternate-unit receipts are converted first.
* No backfill: the engine writes the last (value, quantity) pair from now on and never modifies existing zero-quantity positions; a position without the stored pair falls to source 2 or 3.
* Approval snapshot: item, warehouse, accounting version, quantity on hand, covered and uncovered split, cost source and cost, document revision; any change makes the approval stale.
* An issue with settlements cannot be reversed until its settlements are reversed (newest first); a receipt reversal can still be refused by the engine for later movements. Both are documented behavior.

**Proposals marked for the owner (not decided by the agent):** the obligation account type (liability or contra-inventory), oldest-first matching, the scope of "latest reliable receipt" (item and warehouse, non-reversed, non-zero value), approver different from creator, the soft-close warning, refusal of every other inflow while an obligation is open (R3), and the schema choice of R1.

**Additional tests:** fully uncovered issue, fully matched receipt, another inflow with an open obligation, a receipt dated before the issue, rounding remainders across three partial matches, a change of account O between versions, receipt reversal after Final Close, deactivation attempts, manual entries to O refused.

## Decisions taken under the owner's standing authority (2026-11-16)
* **D1 (R1):** a costing operation of a new kind `provisional` is allowed with zero entries; `StockPosting.cost_operation` stays NOT NULL and unique. Rationale: no change to the 0016 column semantics, the one-row-per-execution rule and the 3.7C reversal traceability stay intact; the only change to existing tables is widening the operation-kind domain (an additive CHECK change) and allowing an operation of that kind to have no entries.
* **D2:** the obligation account O is a configured liability-type account (an accrued/clearing obligation to be settled by a receipt), the conventional conservative treatment for goods issued before receipt; contra-inventory is not used because it would make the inventory asset appear negative.
* **D3:** oldest-first matching by (effective date, sequence), receipts dated on or after the obligation only.
* **D4:** every other inflow into an item and warehouse with an open obligation is refused (R3).
* **D5:** "latest reliable receipt" means the most recent non-reversed receipt entry of that item and warehouse with positive quantity and positive value.
* **D6:** the exception approver must differ from the poster and from the creator by default; a company may additionally require a second distinct approver (configurable).
* **D7:** open obligations at soft close appear as a warning on the closing checklist; at Final Close they block (N3).

**R10 (N2, scope of the schema change; replaces the last sentence of D1).** Exact changes to existing objects, all in one reviewed company migration: (a) the operation-kind CHECK of the costing operation table (`k_f356c89a`) admits kind 5 `provisional`, and the "reversal iff reverses" rule continues to hold; (b) an operation of kind 5 may have zero entries, and **a reversal operation (kind 4) that reverses a kind-5 operation may have zero entries**; the engine keeps refusing empty operations of every other kind; (c) **no column is added to the costing operation table**: the link runs one way, from the new obligation and settlement tables to the operation (no circular reference). `StockPosting.cost_operation` and `StockPostingReversal.compensating_operation` stay NOT NULL and unique.

**R11 (N3, N4: obligation reversal and invariant ordering).** An append-only `StockNegativeObligationReversal` row (obligation, reversing posting, effective date, time, user) records "the originating issue was reversed"; open quantity = original − net matched − (original if reversed). A reversal needs the obligation to have no net settlement. The R3 invariant (open obligation implies position quantity 0) exempts the reversal of the issue that created the obligation, and the write order is: obligation-reversal row, then settlement rows (for receipts), then the position update; the invariant is a deferred constraint trigger evaluated at commit so the order inside the transaction cannot create a false refusal.

**R12 (N5, guarded changes).** While an item has an open obligation the database refuses (triggers, backstop) and the service refuses with `negative_obligation_open`: changing the item's eligibility flag or tracking, moving the item to another group, changing the valuation method policy of any group in its parent chain, and changing the company default valuation method when the resolved method would no longer be moving average; deactivating the item or the warehouse. The concrete table names are taken from `CompanySchema.Inventory.cs` and the valuation policy tables at implementation time and are listed in `docs/PHASE-3.8B-3.md` (as built) with a test per guarded change.

**R13 (N6).** A receipt whose effective date is before an open obligation of the same item and warehouse is **refused** with `negative_obligation_older` (it cannot match and may not enter the position, R3); the engine's no-backdating check does not cover a position without a last date, so the check is explicit and tested.

**R14 (N7, N8).** Uniqueness: a partial unique index on (obligation, receipt posting line) where `reverses_settlement_id` is null, plus a unique `reverses_settlement_id`. When a receipt line is matched in full across several obligations, the receipt remainder goes to the last match (and the provisional remainder to the last match of each obligation).
