# HASM — Phase 3.6: Inventory costing engine (as built)

Sources: SRS §9 and ARCHITECTURE §7–§8 (cost per **item × warehouse**, FIFO and moving weighted average, transfers carry the actual cost, no negative stock by default, atomic stock/valuation/accounting-state commit), DECISIONS D-017/D-018/D-019, `docs/PHASE-3.4.md` (valuation policy master data), `docs/PHASE-3.5.md` (documents up to approval).

**Scope: the costing engine and its persistence only.** It is infrastructure for the posting slice (3.7). Nothing in the application calls it for a document, a screen or a job: the service is deliberately **not registered in the API's dependency injection**, there are no endpoints and no UI, approved stock documents are never touched, no quantity-on-hand screen exists and no accounting entry is created. The engine is exercised only through its transactional API (tests today).

## Architecture and data model (company migration 0014, `CompanySchema.Costing.cs`)
| Table | Role |
|---|---|
| `CostPosition` | one row per item and warehouse: valuation method (fixed at the first entry), quantity, value, last entry sequence and date. The unit of costing **and of locking**. |
| `CostEntry` | the costing ledger: one immutable row per movement of cost through a position (receipt, issue, transfer out/in), direction, exact quantity and amount, the quantity and value **after** it, a per-position sequence, optional `reverses_entry_id`. |
| `CostLayer` | FIFO layers: original and remaining quantity and amount, arrival sequence, optional source layer (transfers). |
| `CostConsumption` | traceability: which layers an outflow consumed (and which a reversal restored), exact quantity and amount. |
| `CostOperation` | one row per request: idempotency **key** (unique), kind, SHA-256 of the request, source reference (traceability only), effective date, actor, reversal links. |

PostgreSQL enforces: no negative quantity or value, no value without quantity, a layer between 0 and what it received (**no negative FIFO layer**), immutable method/item/warehouse, a position moving by exactly one entry at a time, no back-dating, append-only entries/consumptions/operations (an operation can only be marked reversed, once), one reversal per entry and per operation, no deletion or truncation of anything. Runtime role: SELECT/INSERT/UPDATE (UPDATE only on operations, positions and layers) — never DELETE.

## Monetary precision and rounding (the only rule)
Amounts are in the company **base currency** (SRS: ledger in base currency), validated against its **minor units** (USD 2, JOD 3, JPY 0 …). **Receipts are never rounded:** the caller states the exact total amount and the engine refuses more decimals than the currency allows (`amount_precision_exceeded`); zero is allowed (free goods). **Cost is rounded in exactly one place:** when part of a layer or of an average position is issued, `cost = amount × part ÷ whole`, computed **exactly** (big-integer arithmetic on 10⁻⁶ amount units and 10⁻¹⁰ quantity units, never floating point and never 28-digit `decimal` multiplication), then rounded half away from zero to the currency's minor units. When the part is the whole (a layer or the position is emptied) the cost is the **exact remainder**, so the rounding difference always lands on the last unit: a layer's amount and everything issued from it add up exactly, an emptied position ends at exactly zero value, nothing is lost or invented (e.g. 100.00 in thirds issues 33.33 + 33.34 + 33.33). Unit costs are derived (amount ÷ quantity, 10 decimals), display-only, never stored and never used to compute a cost. Quantities are base-unit quantities with at most the base unit's decimals (≤ 10).

## FIFO
A receipt creates a layer (quantity, exact amount). An issue consumes layers **strictly oldest first** by arrival sequence, each fully before the next, the last possibly partially; each consumption is recorded with its layer, quantity and amount, so every issued unit is traceable. The engine plans with the pure function `CostingMath.PlanFifo` (deterministic, never produces a negative layer) and fails with `insufficient_stock` when the layers cannot cover the quantity.

## Moving weighted average
A position holds quantity and value; a receipt adds both; an issue costs `value × issued ÷ onHand` (rounded as above, exact remainder when emptying). Average unit cost = value ÷ quantity (derived). A zero balance has zero value and a later receipt starts afresh.

## Policy, scope and refusals
The method of a position is the one the approved policy (item group chain, then company default) resolves to at its **first entry**, then **fixed**: a later policy change is never applied silently (`valuation_method_changed`; re-costing needs a future controlled revaluation). Refused: services (`item_not_stockable`), lot/serial/expiry items (`tracked_item_not_supported_yet`), undecided policy (`valuation_method_unresolved`), unknown item/warehouse, back-dated entries (`backdated_not_supported`: back-dated movements need the impact preview and recalculation of a later slice), and **negative stock in every form** (`insufficient_stock`): no moving-average exception is implemented. Costing is per **item × warehouse**; company-level figures are aggregations.

## Transfers
`TransferAsync` costs the source with its own method and carries **exactly that value** to the destination: FIFO — one mirrored layer per consumed layer (same quantity and exact amount, same order, `source_layer_id` set; transferred stock queues behind the destination's existing stock); average — the same total added to the destination. The two entries net to zero in quantity-weighted value, so **no artificial profit or loss** and the company total never changes (asserted in tests). Transfers are within one company; no inter-branch settlement.

## Concurrency, transactions, idempotency
* **Transaction boundary:** every operation runs inside a unit of work **owned by the caller** (`CompanyAuditedWork`): the posting transaction will commit the document status, the cost and the accounting state together; any exception rolls back everything (layers, entries, positions, the audit event). `RunAsync` is the single-operation wrapper.
* **Deterministic ordering:** an operation first locks the positions it touches (`SELECT … FOR UPDATE`) in a fixed order (warehouse, item), so opposite transfers cannot deadlock and concurrent operations on one position serialise in lock order; each sees the committed result of the previous; no layer can be consumed twice. Entry and layer sequences are per position, gapless and unique.
* **Idempotency:** the caller's key (≤ 100 chars) plus a hash of the content: same key + same content returns the stored result (`Replayed = true`) and changes nothing; same key + other content/kind/date is `idempotency_key_reused`. Parallel duplicates serialise on the key (advisory lock) so exactly one applies.
* **Corrections and reversals:** nothing is edited. `ReverseAsync` compensates a whole operation: an outflow gets back **exactly** what it consumed (same layers, same amounts; average: same quantity and value); an inflow is taken out again only while its stock is untouched (FIFO: its layer fully remaining; average: every later entry already taken back) — otherwise `reversal_not_possible_goods_consumed` / `…_later_movements`. An operation is reversed at most once, a reversal cannot be reversed, and the reversal is itself idempotent by key. A cost correction is a reversal followed by a new operation; history stays complete.
* **Reconciliation:** `ReconcileAsync` verifies at item, warehouse and company level: position = signed sum of entries and the figures after its last entry; FIFO open layers = position; each layer's consumption = what left it; every outflow/reversal's amount = its consumptions; each transfer moves the same value and quantity out as in; company total value = signed sum of all entries; no negative balances. Tests assert it is empty after every scenario, including a 150-step deterministic random scenario of receipts, issues, transfers and reversals on two warehouses and four items.

## Accounting integration contract (for Phase 3.7; defined and implemented on the costing side only)
* **Inputs the posting slice supplies:** an idempotency key per posting (e.g. `stock_document:<id>`), the effective date, the source reference, and per line the item, warehouse(s) and **base-unit quantity**; for receipts also the exact **amount** in base currency (supplied by the document/purchasing side — a stock receipt document has no cost today and must obtain one before it can be posted).
* **Outputs:** `CostingResult` — per line: entry id, direction, quantity, exact amount, derived unit cost, quantity/value after, and the layer consumptions; `ToValueLines()` — the signed quantity and value change **per item and warehouse** (the basis of the inventory-asset side of a journal entry; the counter side — cost of goods sold, purchase/GRNI, stock adjustment or none for a transfer — is chosen by the posting slice from the document type and the account mappings, which the Inventory module decides); `NetValueChange` (0 for a transfer).
* **Required accounting information (Phase 3.7):** inventory account mapping per item group/warehouse, the counter accounts per document type, the document date's fiscal period (open), the base currency (the engine uses it for precision only), the branch of the movement. The journal must be posted through the existing journal service in the same transaction or left in an explicit *accounting-pending* state (ARCHITECTURE §7), with the cost entry and the journal linked.
* **Transaction boundary:** one `CompanyAuditedWork` for: document → posted, costing operation(s), journal entry (or pending state), audit. Costing is called with the caller's work; the engine never opens its own transaction in that path.
* **Reversal requirements:** cancelling a posted document = `ReverseAsync` of its costing operation(s) + a reversing journal entry, in one transaction; refused with the codes above when the goods were consumed or later movements exist (then the correction path is the recalculation slice).
* **Audit:** one audit event per operation (1150 receipt, 1151 issue, 1152 transfer, 1153 reversal) carrying the key, counts and value; replays add none; the posting slice adds the document's own events.
* **Error handling:** every refusal is a `CostingException` with a stable code (`insufficient_stock`, `valuation_method_unresolved`, `valuation_method_changed`, `tracked_item_not_supported_yet`, `item_not_stockable`, `item_not_found`, `warehouse_not_found`, `backdated_not_supported`, `amount_precision_exceeded`, `quantity_precision_exceeded`, `invalid_quantity`, `invalid_amount`, `invalid_key`, `invalid_lines`, `idempotency_key_reused`, `operation_not_found`, `already_reversed`, `reversal_cannot_be_reversed`, `reversal_not_possible_goods_consumed`, `reversal_not_possible_later_movements`, `same_warehouse`, `duplicate_item_line`, `base_currency_not_set`); all roll the caller's transaction back.

## Compatibility with Phase 3.5
Approved documents stay approved, not posted. No foreign key links a document to a cost table and nothing in the engine or the application reads documents, so **activating posting later cannot silently execute previously approved documents**: Phase 3.7 must add an explicit, authorized *post* action per document (its own permission and revalidation at that moment, idempotent key `stock_document:<id>`), never a sweep over approved documents. A test asserts that approving documents creates no cost data and that the schemas are unlinked.
